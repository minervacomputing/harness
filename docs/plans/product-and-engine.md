# Laravel is the product, Cloudflare is the engine

Minerva is two systems: a private Laravel application, which is the product, and the engine in this repository, which runs the turns. This document holds the decisions that split them. How the engine works is in [ARCHITECTURE.md](../../ARCHITECTURE.md), the internal API between the two in [contract.md](contract.md), and Laravel's side in the app's `docs/architecture/`.

## Why

An earlier design split the product's state between Laravel and the Workers: identity in Laravel; memberships, policies, connections and conversations in the runtime, kept in step by a replicated directory. Its reviews found over forty issues, nearly all at that seam (sessions across two systems, revocation lineage, versioned snapshots, outboxes both ways, recovery logs, reconciliation). That is a distributed system a small team pays for on every feature.

This design splits by responsibility instead: **Laravel decides what may happen; the engine does it and records what it did.** Laravel holds the product's state: who exists, what they connected, what they allowed, what they asked and what they were shown. The engine holds execution facts: what a run did, which writes reached a provider, which files a turn left. Commands go one way, each idempotent; facts come back the other way, through one outbox. Nothing is replicated in both directions, and every admin and support question is answered from Laravel.

The test for every decision is what is best for Minerva as a hosted business. Self-hosting and deployments into a customer's account get worse under this design, and that is accepted.

## Decisions

1. **Laravel is the product.** A private Laravel application on Laravel Cloud, with Postgres, owns users, sign-in, organizations, roles, billing and administration; connections and their stored credentials; grants and layers as users set them; agents; the product transcript; the run registry; quotas, usage and the audit log.
2. **The engine executes, and owns execution facts.** The Cloudflare code in this repository (`cloudflare/`) runs a turn: pi's loop in the Conversation object, the sandbox, code mode, the connectors' calls under the permission check, and the live stream. It keeps, durably: each conversation's folder (its versions and manifests in the Conversation object, blobs in R2); each run's input, attempts, write ledger, page tokens and run events; the conversation's pi conversation; an outbox of facts for Laravel; and a recovery index in D1. The index holds a row for each run, written before the Conversation object admits it (`startRun` writes it first and is idempotent by run id, so execution never begins without it), and for each conversation the highest fact sequence it has, raised before a batch is delivered. Its rows outlive the restore window, deleted conversations' included. These are the authority on what a run did; Laravel's transcript is built from them (Decisions 7). The engine holds conversation content while it works and for a retention period, but no product transcript, no users, sessions or directory, and no stored credentials. A container is the conversation's: started when a run's folder has files or its first local call needs one, filled from the folder's last version, saved when the run ends, then parked for 5 minutes for the conversation's next turn, which takes it when it holds that turn's folder; otherwise destroyed. The folder in R2 stays the record, and a container can always be rebuilt from it.
3. **Connector knowledge and policy stay in TypeScript.** The connectors and the permission pipeline stay in the engine: tools, scopes, resource kinds, grant semantics, scope checks, tool aliases, operation fingerprints, and compiling a run's policy snapshot (`gateway/tools.ts`, `gateway/grants.ts`). Laravel stores the raw inputs (layers, grants, agents' connections, each with a revision) and never interprets them. The engine publishes a versioned catalog: connectors, actions and their scopes, resource kinds, and each connector's complete OAuth descriptor (endpoints, the token request's body format, client authentication, scope separator, extra authorization parameters, PKCE, dynamic registration, refresh behaviour).
4. **Connections live in Laravel.**
   - **The flow.** `league/oauth2-client`'s generic provider, driven by the catalog's descriptor, with an adapter only where a descriptor cannot express a provider (none needs one). Dynamic client registration where a connector has it (Todoist). After the exchange Laravel asks the engine to describe the account (`describeAccount`: id, label, and the tenant where the connector knows one) and stores it. Reconnecting, or replacing a key, must name the same provider and account as the connection; a different account becomes a new connection with no grants, never new credentials under the old one's grants.
   - **Stored.** The tokens; the client that issued them and the token endpoint (a refresh uses the same); scopes; generation; refresh state; the account. Encrypted with a key of their own, not `APP_KEY`, with key ids for rotation; the plaintext also carries the organization, the connection and the purpose, checked on every decrypt, so a ciphertext copied to another row does not open.
   - **Refresh.** Its states are persisted: `refreshing` is committed with an operation id, an owner and an expiry before the request is sent; other callers wait for it; a takeover after the expiry, or an answer that was lost, makes the connection `refresh_uncertain`; a result is applied only if the operation id, its owner and the generation are still the ones recorded, since a takeover does not change the generation. A lock or a queued job that expired never makes a retry safe by itself.
   - **What the engine gets.** Never a refresh token, a client secret or the key. `leaseToken` (run, attempt, connection) answers the credential's kind, the secret (an access token, an API key, or a built-in service's), its scopes, its generation and its expiry when known; Laravel answers only for a run that is starting or running, and for a connection in that run's authorized set. The settings calls Laravel makes itself (`describeAccount`, `listResources`, `checkGrantEdit`) carry their credential in the command instead, freshly refreshed, so the engine never asks back for them (contract.md, Credentials in settings calls). A run's provider rejection is reported with `reportUnauthorized` (run, attempt, connection, generation), fenced by the generation; a settings call answers `CREDENTIAL_REFUSED`. The engine keeps credentials in memory only, and checks the run and attempt before and after each lease.
   - **The bound this gives.** A compromised engine can use the credentials it was handed until the provider expires or revokes them (API keys and some tokens never expire), and can collect more while it stays compromised and runs keep starting. It never sees refresh tokens, nor the credentials of connections that no run and no settings call used. This answers part of ARCHITECTURE.md's open question 1; keeping credentials out of the loop's Worker entirely would need a separate executor.
5. **The interface.** Declared once in TypeScript (the engine's oRPC contract in `contract/`) with an OpenAPI document Laravel's client follows. Each version stays served until both sides have moved past it; a compatibility suite runs Laravel's client against the engine's contract.
   - **Commands, Laravel to the engine**, idempotent by their id: `startRun`, `cancelRun`, `describeAccount`, `listResources`, `checkGrantEdit`, `folder` (a conversation's listing at a version), `resolveDownload` (a file of a version: its R2 key, size and hash), `deleteConversation`, `replayFacts` (a conversation's retained facts after a sequence, for recovery), `listConversations` (the recovery index, for a restore), and the catalog. Every command about a conversation names the environment, organization, workspace and conversation; an execution object stores the organization and workspace it was created for and refuses a command that names others.
   - **Requests and facts, the engine to Laravel:** `leaseToken`, `reportUnauthorized`, and the outbox's deliveries (Decisions 7).
   - **Authentication.** Laravel's calls carry a Cloudflare Access service token; Access checks it and the Worker checks the assertion again (issuer, audience, the service token's `common_name`). The engine's calls carry a Sanctum token whose abilities allow only these routes. Both are rotated with an overlap.
6. **Starting a run, and strict revocation.**
   - **Start.** In one transaction holding the workspace's row lock (`SELECT … FOR UPDATE`), Laravel checks that the conversation has no active run, the agent, the uploads and the limits; reserves the uploads; captures the raw authority inputs with their revisions; registers the run as `starting` with the user's message; and writes the start to its outbox. A queued job sends `startRun` with the same run id until it is answered. The engine compiles the snapshot, copies the attachments into the folder, and accepts or refuses. It compiles once: the snapshot is stored under the run id, and a repeated `startRun` gets the same answer. An acceptance names the catalog's and the compiler's versions, the snapshot's digest and the run's authorized set (the connections the snapshot can reach), which Laravel stores with the run. Until then the authorized set is the agent's connections, read from the rows without interpreting any grant; Laravel never evaluates grants itself. The page shows the message as sending until then; a refusal marks the run refused, shows the message as not sent with the reason, and releases the reservations, so the message stays visible as failed, as in a chat app. A start still unanswered after a bound is cancelled instead (`cancelRun` tombstones it, so it can never begin), and its reservations are released only once that is acknowledged.
   - **Ordering.** Every change of authority (a grant or layer edit, a removed connection or reduced scope, an agent's connections, a removed member, the emergency stop) takes the same lock, so a start and a revocation are ordered: a start committed first is found by the revocation's scan of active runs, and one committed after it captures the new inputs. This is tested with concurrent processes against a real Postgres.
   - **Revocation.** The change commits with a cancel entry for each affected run that is starting or running: for a removed connection or a reduced scope, every run whose authorized set holds that connection, whoever started it; for a removed member, their runs; for a layer, the runs of every subject it constrains (a ceiling: all runs using the connection; a user's layer: that user's; an agent's: that agent's). A test with two users on a shared connection covers it. A queued job sends `cancelRun`; the engine acknowledges once the Conversation object has recorded the cancellation durably, or a tombstone for a run it has not received yet, so a late start is refused. Every provider request checks that fence immediately before it is sent (`admits` in `gateway/executor.ts`). A request already sent is not undone; a write in flight settles and is recorded. The change is answered once every cancel is acknowledged; past a bound it answers that it is pending (a 503) and the job keeps going. Refusing `leaseToken` is a second guard, not the enforcement.
7. **Conversations and the transcript.**
   - **Facts.** In the same transaction as the execution facts they describe, the engine writes product facts to its outbox: a run's status changes; each tool call as the page shows it (name, truncated arguments, outcome, counts, never a provider's full result); the final message; the run's resulting folder version and changed files; write settlements, including those that settle after a cancellation, for the audit log; and usage per model call.
   - **Delivery.** In order per conversation, with a sequence number. Laravel applies a batch in one transaction and acknowledges only then; a repeat is applied once, and a repeat whose content differs stops that conversation's delivery and alerts the operator. Acknowledged facts stay in the engine through the restore window, so `replayFacts` can send them again. The engine never deletes an unacknowledged fact; a backlog past a bound refuses new starts in that conversation rather than dropping anything.
   - **History.** A conversation has one active run. Laravel marks a run finished only from its terminal fact, which carries the final message and folder version, so a next start always sees the whole previous turn. Each conversation has one pi conversation, seeded by the conversation's first run from the recent messages Laravel sends with `startRun` (the engine applies `history.ts`'s selection and clipping), then continued by every later run and compacted by pi near the context window. Laravel sends `history` only until a run of the conversation has completed, since an earlier run that failed or was cancelled may have ended before the engine made the pi conversation.
   - **Deltas** are not facts: they are the engine's run events, streamed live and replayed to a reconnecting browser for the run's retention period, and never sent to Laravel.
   - **Search** is Laravel Scout on Postgres full-text search; semantic search later with pgvector.
8. **The browser.** The product's pages are Laravel's: Inertia with React (first-party), with the Ledger design system. Laravel serves the pages and their data in one request.
   - **The live stream** is read from the engine over SSE, with a token Laravel signs for one run that names the environment, organization, conversation and run, its audience and purpose, and lives at most five minutes. The browser reads it with `fetch` and an `Authorization` header, not `EventSource`, so the token stays out of URLs and logs. The engine closes the stream when the token expires; the browser gets a new one from Laravel, which checks the membership again, and resumes with the last event id. A removed member therefore loses a stream within one token's life.
   - **Uploads** go from the browser to a staging key in R2 with Laravel's `Storage::temporaryUploadUrl`: a random key, a short expiry, the declared size and type signed into the request, so R2 refuses a PUT that differs. Laravel reserves the size first. At `startRun` the engine copies the staging object into the folder, computing its size and hash itself and refusing a mismatch; staging objects are deleted after the run, or after a day if none used them.
   - **Downloads.** Laravel asks `resolveDownload` and signs a GET for that key, with the content disposition that forces a download, on R2's own origin. Laravel never derives an R2 key from a path.
9. **Packages.** Laravel's React starter kit (Inertia, React, Fortify: sign-in, two-factor, passkeys, verification, reset), Socialite (Google, GitHub; Microsoft through the SocialiteProviders adapter), Sanctum, Cashier, Pennant, Scout, Filament for the operator admin, Nightwatch, queues and the scheduler on Laravel Cloud, `league/oauth2-client` for connections; WorkOS for enterprise SSO later. Organizations, members and invitations are the application's own models.
10. **Postgres, not MySQL.** Postgres has pgvector, better full-text search and JSON columns, and Laravel Cloud offers it.
11. **Hosted only.** Minerva Cloud runs one Laravel application and one engine deployment. Self-hosting, the hybrid (our Laravel, the engine in a customer's account) and cells are not offered. An enterprise that needs its own deployment later gets a dedicated copy of both. Laravel runs in Frankfurt and the engine's storage in the EU jurisdiction, but that is a deployment, not a residency promise: where the Workers, containers, models, logs, backups and telemetry process data is documented in [docs/data-processing.md](../data-processing.md), and Minerva promises EU processing only when all of them allow it.

## Deletion, retention and recovery

**Deletion.** Deleting a conversation commits a tombstone and a deletion entry in Laravel's outbox; the transcript is hidden at once. `deleteConversation` makes the engine tombstone the conversation, refuse starts and writes, cancel its run, and clean up; it acknowledges. A late fact for a tombstoned conversation never brings its content back: transcript facts are acknowledged and dropped, while usage and write settlements are applied to the run's accounting and audit records, which are kept under their own retention rather than the conversation's. Rows and objects are removed physically after the restore window; tombstones outlive it. A conversation's container snapshot cannot be deleted: the engine forgets its record at the deletion, so nothing restores it, and Cloudflare expires it 30 days after its last restore (ARCHITECTURE.md, Retiring and snapshots). Deleting an organization does the same for each of its conversations and connections.

**Retention.** Every record that deduplicates (run ids, command ids, cancel tombstones, acknowledged sequence numbers) is kept longer than any retry can arrive; retries stop after a bound. The write ledger is kept for as long as a run can resume. The engine's run events and run inputs are deleted after the run's retention period; a conversation's pi conversation is kept until the conversation is deleted (ARCHITECTURE.md, open question 2); facts never before they are acknowledged. The periods are under Settled questions.

**Recovery.** Restoring is still a reconciliation, because the engine and the providers move on while Laravel is restored. The rules:

- **Reductions are written ahead.** Before Laravel commits a change that reduces access, or a deletion, it appends a restriction to a log in R2 with a bucket lock. A restriction is not the edit: it is a removal, by stable ids. A restore applies every restriction logged after its point. Each only ever removes access, so one whose change never committed is harmless, and their order does not matter. The log covers a member's removal or lowered role, a connection's revocation or narrowed reconnection, an invitation's revocation, an admin's removal, a sign-out, sign-in credentials removed or changed, and the deletion of an account, a workspace or an organization. Grant edits and layers are not logged, and conversation deletions are answered by the engine's listing.
- **Everything stops** while Laravel is restored and reconciled: admission is closed, every active run is cancelled, and no lease is answered.
- **Runs are reconciled from the engine.** Laravel pages through the engine's whole recovery index (`listConversations`) and compares each row with what it holds: it deletes again a conversation the engine has deleted, replays (`replayFacts`) one whose fact sequence is above its watermark, and records the usage of one it does not hold. Late usage and settlements of runs that had already ended are found the same way. The engine is not rolled back with Laravel: its write ledgers and facts record what reached providers, which no restore undoes. A Durable Object is never rolled back either: a broken Conversation object is repaired forward, or its conversation deleted.
- **Credentials.** A restored OAuth connection with a refresh token is marked `refresh_uncertain`, since the provider may have rotated it since the point. API keys and built-in services are left as they are; a restore cannot make them stale.
- **Files.** Blobs are never overwritten (their keys hold a hash and a random part), and removed only after the restore window; the manifests are in the Conversation objects, which are not rolled back.
- **Money.** Usage facts are kept by the engine until acknowledged, so a restored Laravel receives them again. Billing is by seats, so the restore runbook runs `minerva:billing:sync` before serving.
- **The key.** The credentials' encryption key is backed up outside Laravel Cloud, since a restored database is useless without it.

## Settled questions

- **The stream token** is an HS256 JWT that Laravel signs with `firebase/php-jwt` under `MINERVA_STREAM_KEYS` and the engine checks with `jose`. Laravel Reverb was rejected: it would need its own way to close subscriptions that are already open when a member is removed.
- **Postgres** is Laravel Cloud's Serverless Postgres (Neon), Postgres 18 in `eu-central-1`; pgvector 0.8 is available and Postgres's own full-text search needs no extension. A cluster at 0.25 compute units reports `max_connections` 112, and its PgBouncer pooler (transaction mode) takes up to 10,000 clients, for queue workers. TLS is verified against Neon's own host name, since its certificate does not name the alias Laravel Cloud injects.
- **Open source.** This repository stays public as Minerva's open harness, a trust signal for a product that holds people's accounts, and holds the engine alone.
- **The credentials key.** Laravel's encrypter (AES-256-GCM) under a key of its own, `MINERVA_CREDENTIALS_KEY`, not `APP_KEY` (`App\Connections\Vault`). A key service can come later without changing what is stored.
- **The operator admin** is Filament 5, free and in the same Livewire stack as the rest of Laravel's first-party tools; Nova's licence buys nothing a read-mostly operator panel needs.
- **Where data is processed**, and the subprocessors, are in [docs/data-processing.md](../data-processing.md). Minerva may promise EU storage of its primary data with those exceptions named, not EU processing.
- **Billing** is per seat at a flat monthly price, with each seat's allowance of the engine's charges pooled per organization, and no usage billed: Cashier with Checkout, the Customer Portal and Stripe's webhooks, and Stripe's subscription sets the seats.
- **The cost of a typical turn** is about $0.02 on `gpt-6.1-sol` and $0.002 on GLM, at list prices. A stopped call is charged at the route's limits, about 15 typical turns on `gpt-6.1-sol`.
- **Retention periods.** A restore window of 7 days; run events and run inputs kept 30 days after the run, and the conversation's pi conversation until the conversation is deleted; facts 7 days after acknowledgement; transcripts and files until deletion, and deleted conversations removed physically 7 days later; usage and audit at least a year. The budget's unit is money.
